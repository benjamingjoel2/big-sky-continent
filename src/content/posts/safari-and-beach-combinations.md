The last game drive ends the way they all do: engine off, the sky going from gold to violet, a hyena calling somewhere beyond the thornbush. You have spent days with dust in your hair and a 5am alarm in your bones. Then, a short flight later, the window fills with a colour you had forgotten existed. Turquoise. The door opens onto warm, salty air, and the only schedule left is the tide.

That is the promise of a safari-and-beach trip. The safari gives you the adrenaline; the coast gives you time to let it sink in. But the pairings are not interchangeable: each has its own seasons, border crossings and paperwork. Here is how the classic combinations fit together.

If this is your first time in the bush, start with our guide to [your first African safari](/stories/first-african-safari/), then come back here to add the sand.

## Which order: safari first, beach second

The usual advice is simple: do the safari first. Game drives are early, active and absorbing, and you want to arrive fresh for them. The beach is where you recover, sort through your photographs and sleep past sunrise without guilt. Finishing on the coast also gives you a buffer before the long flight home, so a delayed connection eats into beach time rather than your days in the bush.

## Serengeti and northern Tanzania with Zanzibar

This is the classic for good reason. The Serengeti, the Ngorongoro Crater and the parks of northern Tanzania are a short hop from the Indian Ocean, and Zanzibar is the obvious finale: spice-scented Stone Town, dhows on the horizon, and long beaches where the tide pulls back across the shallows.

**Connecting.** Light aircraft link safari airstrips across northern Tanzania with Arusha and the coast, and many travellers fly straight from the bush to Zanzibar, sometimes with a change on the way. Routes change by season, so plan the connection with whoever books your flights.

**Seasons.** Zanzibar sits in Tanzania's two-rainy-season zone. The Tanzania Meteorological Authority describes the Masika, or long rains, as the March to May season over the northern coast, including Unguja (Zanzibar's main island) and Pemba ([TMA seasonal bulletin](https://wamis.org/wamis/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/TMA-Seasonal-Agromet-bulletin-for-March-to-May-2024-MAM.pdf)). The Vuli, or short rains, normally fall between October and December. For this year specifically, the authority expected the October to December 2026 Vuli to be strongly influenced by El Niño, with average to above-average rain in several areas, possibly running into January 2027 ([Daily News, September 2026](https://dailynews.co.tz/zanzibar-cannot-afford-to-ignore-repeated-warnings-as-el-nino-rains/)). If you are travelling over the coming weeks, build in flexibility.

**Entry and insurance.** British visitors need a visa for Tanzania, and the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) advises applying for an e-visa before you travel. Crucially, Tanzania now requires mandatory inbound travel insurance for all non-resident visitors to both the mainland and Zanzibar, even if you already hold your own policy. You buy it once, for your first port of entry: from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation if you arrive in Zanzibar first, or from the National Insurance Corporation if you land on the mainland first, and you may be refused entry without it ([FCDO Tanzania entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/tanzania/entry-requirements), checked November 2026). Keep your own comprehensive travel insurance as well; the mandatory cover does not replace it. The FCDO's only area-specific warning for Tanzania concerns the border zone with Mozambique's Cabo Delgado province, far from the northern parks and Zanzibar ([FCDO Tanzania warnings](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/tanzania/warnings-and-insurance), checked November 2026).

## Kenya's parks with the Kenyan coast

Kenya offers the same rhythm with a slightly different flavour. After the Maasai Mara or the other great reserves, you can choose between the long white beaches south of Mombasa at Diani, the reef-fringed bays of Watamu further north, or the car-free Swahili lanes of Lamu.

**Connecting.** Light aircraft and scheduled domestic flights link the main safari areas with Nairobi and the coast. As with Tanzania, think in terms of one or two short flights rather than a long road transfer.

**Seasons.** The Kenya Meteorological Department frames the year around two rainy seasons: the "long rains" from March to May and the "short rains" from October to December ([KMD climate outlook](https://meteo.go.ke/documents/2537/March-April-May_MAM_2026_Seasonal_Weather_Forecast.pdf)). The months between are generally the drier choice for combining bush and beach.

**Safety on the coast.** Check FCDO advice carefully for the northern coast. As of November 2026, the FCDO advises against all travel to Lamu County, except for Lamu Island and Manda Island, which are excluded from that warning. It also advises against all but essential travel to areas within 15km of the coast between the Tana River and the Galana (Athi-Galana-Sabaki) River ([FCDO Kenya warnings](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/kenya/warnings-and-insurance), updated 6 November 2026, checked November 2026). If you want Lamu, that means keeping to the islands themselves rather than travelling through the mainland county, and checking the FCDO's map to see exactly where any other coastal stay falls. Diani and Watamu are not named in that advice, but read the latest version before you book.

**Entry.** British visitors must apply online for an electronic travel authorisation before travelling, ideally at least two weeks ahead, through Kenya's official eTA portal, which the FCDO links to. Since 5 October 2026, most visitors must also buy Kenyan government-provided travel health insurance as part of the eTA application; foreign policies are not accepted for this requirement ([FCDO Kenya entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/kenya/entry-requirements), checked November 2026).

## Kruger or Sabi Sand with Mozambique

For a southern African version, pair South Africa's Kruger National Park, or one of the private reserves on its western edge such as the Sabi Sand, with the Mozambican coast. The Bazaruto Archipelago, reached from the mainland town of Vilanculos, is the classic choice: dunes, dhows and clear shallow water.

**Connecting.** The usual approach is a regional flight, often via Johannesburg, then a boat or light-aircraft transfer out to the islands. Some travellers drive between Kruger and southern Mozambique, but flying is the simplest way to reach Vilanculos and Bazaruto.

**Seasons.** The FCDO notes that cyclones and floods are common in Mozambique during the rainy season from November to April, and that low-lying areas near major rivers can flood at short notice ([FCDO Mozambique safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mozambique/safety-and-security), checked November 2026). The drier months from May to October are the steadier bet for this combination.

**Safety.** The FCDO's warnings for Mozambique concern the far north: Cabo Delgado province (except Palma town, where it advises against all but essential travel), the Memba and Eráti districts of Nampula, and the Mecula and Marrupa districts of Niassa ([FCDO Mozambique warnings](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mozambique/warnings-and-insurance), checked November 2026). Inhambane province, home to Vilanculos and Bazaruto, is not among them, but check before you travel.

**Entry.** British visitors to Mozambique need an Electronic Travel Authorisation for stays of up to 30 days. Apply online at least five working days before travel; you cannot pay on arrival ([FCDO Mozambique entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mozambique/entry-requirements), checked November 2026). For South Africa, British visitors do not need a visa for stays of up to 90 days, but your passport needs at least two blank pages and must be valid for at least 30 days after you leave ([FCDO South Africa entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/south-africa/entry-requirements), checked November 2026). Since 1 July 2026, anyone entering or leaving South Africa must also submit an online traveller declaration to the South African Revenue Service, no more than 24 hours before departure ([SARS FAQs](https://www.sars.gov.za/travellerdeclaration/faqs-for-the-required-online-traveller-declarations-from-1-july-2026/), checked November 2026). That applies each time you cross, so set a reminder before your Mozambique-to-South Africa flight home.

## Southern Africa with Mauritius or Seychelles

If you want a proper island finale, fly out to the Indian Ocean. Mauritius brings lagoons, sugar-cane valleys and Creole cooking; the Seychelles offer granite boulders, forested peaks and island-hopping between Mahé, Praslin and La Digue. Both pair well with a safari in South Africa or Botswana, and our [14-day Southern Africa itinerary](/stories/a-14-day-southern-africa-itinerary/) can be extended with a week on either.

**Connecting.** Expect to fly via a regional hub, most commonly Johannesburg, rather than directly from a safari airstrip.

**Seasons in Mauritius.** Mauritius Meteorological Services describes a warm, humid summer from November to April and a cooler, drier winter from June to September, with October and May as transition months. It notes there is no marked rainy season, though February and March are the wettest months ([Mauritius Meteorological Services](https://metservice.intnet.mu/climate-services/climate-of-mauritius.php)). The FCDO says the tropical cyclone season usually runs from November to May ([FCDO Mauritius safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mauritius/safety-and-security), checked November 2026).

**Seasons in the Seychelles.** The islands swing between two wind seasons. The FCDO warns that west-coast beaches are unsafe for swimming during the north-west monsoon, around December to March, and east-coast beaches during the south-east winds, around May to September. Heavy rain is most likely from November to March ([FCDO Seychelles safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/seychelles/safety-and-security), checked November 2026). The upshot: whatever the month, pick a beach on the sheltered side.

**Entry.** British visitors can stay in Mauritius without a visa for up to 60 days, need proof of onward travel, and should complete the Mauritius All in One travel form before arrival ([FCDO Mauritius entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mauritius/entry-requirements), checked November 2026). The Seychelles do not require a visa, but you must apply and pay for a travel authorisation online before travelling, and carry proof of onward travel and confirmed accommodation ([FCDO Seychelles entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/seychelles/entry-requirements), checked November 2026). Both countries also have entry restrictions for travellers who have recently been in the Democratic Republic of Congo (and, for Mauritius, South Sudan), so read the current rules if your route passes through central Africa.

## Lake Malawi: the freshwater alternative

Not every beach needs salt. Lake Malawi is a vast inland sea of clear water, fishing villages and quiet sandy coves, and it pairs naturally with Malawi's own parks or with a safari in Zambia's Luangwa Valley next door.

**Health.** The CDC notes that schistosomiasis, a parasitic infection spread in fresh water, is found in Malawi, and advises avoiding swimming in fresh, unchlorinated water such as lakes. It also recommends malaria prevention medicine for travel anywhere in the country ([CDC Malawi](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/malawi), checked November 2026). Talk to a travel health clinic before you go, and read our guide to [malaria and health in Africa](/stories/malaria-vaccines-health-africa/).

**Entry.** British visitors need a visa for Malawi, available online or on arrival, and must complete a Traveller Health Check form ([FCDO Malawi entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malawi/entry-requirements), checked November 2026).

## Borders, vaccinations and the paperwork in between

The beauty of these combinations is that they often cross a border, and the catch is the same. A few things to keep in mind:

- **Yellow fever.** Most of the countries above require a yellow fever certificate if you arrive from a country listed as a transmission risk, so check each leg in the order you will fly it, using the FCDO pages linked above.
- **Blank pages.** Several countries require two or more blank passport pages. With three or four entry stamps on one trip, they fill up quickly.
- **Insurance stacking.** Tanzania and Kenya now each require their own government insurance on top of your personal policy.
- **Re-check.** Entry rules in this region have changed several times in 2026 alone. Everything here was checked in November 2026, but read the official pages again in the weeks before you travel.

Get those details right and the rest is simple: bush first, then the sea, and a last evening watching the light go soft over the water while that hyena's call settles into memory.
