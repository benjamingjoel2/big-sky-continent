You hear it before you see it. A low, constant roar from somewhere beyond the rainforest path, and then the air itself turns wet. Spray drifts across the trail like fine rain falling upwards. You step out to the edge, and there it is: a vast curtain of water dropping into a black basalt gorge, a rainbow hanging in the mist, and the whole of the Zambezi seeming to vanish into the earth.

Two weeks earlier, you were watching the sun set over the Atlantic from a Cape Town beach. In between, you will have tracked big game and crossed several borders. This is Southern Africa's great loop.

Here is a day-by-day plan, with honest notes on where to slow down, what to swap and when to go.

## The shape of the trip

- **Days 1 to 4:** Cape Town, the Cape Peninsula and an optional Winelands day
- **Days 5 to 8:** Kruger or a private reserve, or Botswana's Okavango Delta
- **Days 9 to 11:** Victoria Falls
- **Days 12 to 14:** Chobe National Park, Botswana

Four bases in fourteen days is full. See the notes on pace further down before you commit.

## Days 1 to 4: Cape Town and the Cape Peninsula

Start slowly. Cape Town is the perfect place to recover from a long flight: a city pinned between mountain and ocean.

**Day 1** is for arriving, walking the waterfront or the colourful streets of the Bo-Kaap, and an early night.

**Day 2** belongs to the mountain. Take the cableway or hike up if you are fit and the weather is clear; the summit can disappear under its famous "tablecloth" of cloud with little warning, so go when the sky is on your side.

**Day 3** is the Cape Peninsula drive: the coast road south, the penguins at Boulders Beach, and the windswept cliffs of Cape Point at the end of the peninsula.

**Day 4** is your choice. Many travellers spend it in the Winelands around Stellenbosch and Franschhoek, an easy day trip from the city with mountain views, Cape Dutch farmhouses and long lunches. Others prefer Robben Island, a township tour with a local guide, or simply a day on the beach.

**When to go.** The City of Cape Town describes its climate as Mediterranean, with warm summers and rainy winters; summer, from December to February, is usually hot and sunny, and winter, from June to August, is the rainy season ([City of Cape Town](https://www.capetown.gov.za/work-and-business/invest-in-cape-town/why-invest-in-cape-town/the-liveability-of-cape-town/)). That matters because the best months for the safari half of this trip fall in the southern winter, so you may be trading a little Cape rain for better game viewing further north. Pack a waterproof and accept it.

## Days 5 to 8: Kruger, a private reserve or the Okavango

This is the big decision, and both options are excellent.

**Option one: Kruger or a private reserve.** Fly from Cape Town to an airport near Kruger, or to an airstrip in one of the private reserves on the park's western edge, such as the Sabi Sand. Kruger itself is vast and suits self-drivers and those who enjoy the freedom of exploring at their own pace. The private reserves offer open-vehicle drives with a guide and tracker, off-road driving to follow sightings, and night drives. If this is your first safari, read our guide to [your first African safari](/stories/first-african-safari/).

**Option two: the Okavango Delta.** Fly north into Botswana, often via Johannesburg to Maun, then by light aircraft to a camp in the Delta. Here the experience is quieter and more watery: mokoro (dugout canoe) trips through papyrus channels, walking on islands, and game viewing in one of the most remarkable wetlands on earth.

The Okavango's flood is the key to timing it. The water comes not from local rain but from rainfall in the highlands of Angola, and UNESCO notes that levels typically peak between June and August, during the region's dry winter months, when the Delta draws exceptional concentrations of wildlife ([UNESCO/IPACC](https://www.unesco.org/sites/default/files/medias/fichiers/2025/04/UNESCO-IPACC-Pub_IndigenousPeoplesWaterAfrica_EN.pdf)). Botswana's government newspaper reports that the inflow normally reaches Boro and Maun around mid-May or the beginning of June ([Botswana Daily News](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/74199)). The timing varies from year to year with the Angolan rains, so treat it as a pattern, not a promise.

Choosing the Okavango has one practical bonus: it puts you in Botswana already, so the onward route to Victoria Falls and Chobe is shorter and can be done by light aircraft. Choosing Kruger usually means flying back through Johannesburg to reach Victoria Falls.

## Days 9 to 11: Victoria Falls

The Falls, known locally as Mosi-oa-Tunya, sit on the Zambezi between Zimbabwe and Zambia, and you can base yourself on either side: the town of Victoria Falls in Zimbabwe or Livingstone in Zambia.

Give yourself three nights. **Day 9** is arrival and a first walk along the rainforest paths opposite the Falls. **Day 10** is for the full experience: the walks on both sides if your visa allows, a helicopter flight if you want to see the scale of the gorges from above, or the rafting and gorge activities the Zambezi is famous for. **Day 11** might be a gentle sunset cruise on the upper Zambezi, above the lip of the Falls, with hippos grunting in the shallows.

**What the Falls look like depends entirely on when you go.** Zambia Tourism describes the peak flood around March and April, when you see the Falls at full power but the sheer volume of spray makes it impossible to see the full width on foot, and you will want rain gear. As the flood eases, the view improves through the year until about September. By November and December, at the height of the dry season, the flow on some stretches shrinks to small rivulets and some parts carry no water at all, revealing the basalt cliffs and the depth of the gorge ([Zambia Tourism](https://www.zambiatourism.com/destinations/waterfalls/victoria-falls/)). There is no wrong time, but there is a different Falls in every season.

## Days 12 to 14: Chobe National Park

From Victoria Falls, it is a short road transfer to Kasane in Botswana, the gateway to Chobe National Park. Crossing at Kazungula, you leave Zimbabwe and enter Botswana in the same morning.

Chobe's riverfront is the classic draw, and the great pleasure here is a boat safari: drifting slowly along the Chobe River, watching the banks and floodplain for elephants, buffalo, hippos and crocodiles, with fish eagles calling overhead. Combine boat trips with game drives along the riverfront, and spend your final evening watching the sun go down over the floodplain.

On **Day 14**, transfer back towards Victoria Falls or fly out from Kasane, depending on your international connection.

## Honest notes on pace and alternatives

Fourteen days, four bases and up to five border crossings is a lot. Every move costs at least half a day, and the safari days start early. Some ways to ease the pace:

- **Drop one stop.** If you choose the Okavango, consider skipping Chobe and spending longer in the Delta, or adding a night at the Falls instead.
- **Choose one country for the bush.** Kruger plus Cape Town is a beautifully simple trip on its own. Okavango plus Chobe plus Victoria Falls makes a superb, almost seamless circuit without South Africa.
- **Add a beach.** If you can stretch to three weeks, a week on the Indian Ocean coast is the perfect finale. See our guide to [safari and beach combinations](/stories/safari-and-beach-combinations/).

## Borders and visas

This route crosses between South Africa, Botswana, Zimbabwe and possibly Zambia, so the paperwork deserves an evening of attention. Everything below was checked in November 2026 for British passport holders; re-check before you travel, and check your own country's rules if you hold a different passport.

**South Africa.** British visitors do not need a visa for stays of up to 90 days. Your passport needs an expiry date at least 30 days after you leave, and at least two blank pages ([FCDO South Africa](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/south-africa/entry-requirements)). Since 1 July 2026, everyone entering or leaving South Africa must submit an online traveller declaration through the South African Revenue Service, no more than 24 hours before departure ([SARS FAQs](https://www.sars.gov.za/travellerdeclaration/faqs-for-the-required-online-traveller-declarations-from-1-july-2026/)).

**Children and South Africa.** The FCDO says special requirements apply to children aged 17 and under. The South African Government's guidance says an unabridged birth certificate, or an equivalent document showing parentage, is required in most cases, with extra documents such as a parental consent affidavit if a child travels with only one parent or another adult ([South African Government](https://www.gov.za/faq/travel-tourism/what-are-requirements-travelling-children)). Exemptions exist, but the rules have changed before; confirm the current position with the Department of Home Affairs or the South African High Commission before you fly.

**Botswana.** British visitors can stay up to 90 days without a visa. Your passport needs at least three blank pages and six months' validity from arrival. Botswana also requires any child aged 17 or under to carry the original or a certified copy of their full birth certificate, plus consent documents if they are not travelling with both parents ([FCDO Botswana](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/botswana/entry-requirements)).

**Zimbabwe.** British visitors need a visa, and most get it on arrival; bring US dollars in small notes for the fee, or apply online in advance. Your passport needs six months' validity, and the FCDO advises three blank pages in case you exit through a neighbouring country ([FCDO Zimbabwe](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/zimbabwe/entry-requirements)).

**Zambia.** British passport holders do not need a visa for tourist stays of up to 90 days in any 12-month period ([FCDO Zambia](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/zambia/entry-requirements)).

**The KAZA UNIVISA.** As of November 2026, the KAZA UNIVISA is still listed as available by both the FCDO and Zambia Tourism. It is valid for 30 days, covers travel between Zimbabwe and Zambia, and allows day trips into Botswana through the Kazungula border. The FCDO's Zimbabwe page says it is issued at Harare, Bulawayo and Victoria Falls airports and at the Victoria Falls and Kazungula land borders; Zambia Tourism lists Lusaka and Livingstone airports and the Victoria Falls and Kazungula border posts ([FCDO Zimbabwe](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/zimbabwe/entry-requirements); [FCDO Zambia](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/zambia/entry-requirements); [Zambia Tourism](https://www.zambiatourism.com/travel-info/visa-information)). It is most useful if you want to see the Falls from both sides. Note that it does not cover an overnight stay in Botswana, so for the Chobe leg you enter Botswana normally.

## Malaria: which parts are risky

Cape Town is not a malaria area, but much of the rest of this route is. The CDC lists Kruger National Park among South Africa's malaria transmission areas and recommends preventive medication there ([CDC South Africa](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/south-africa)). In Botswana, it lists Chobe and the Okavango as transmission areas and recommends prescription medicine ([CDC Botswana](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/botswana)). For Zimbabwe, the CDC lists the whole country as a transmission area and recommends preventive medicine for all travellers ([CDC Zimbabwe](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/zimbabwe)). All checked November 2026.

Book a travel health appointment well before you go, take your antimalarials exactly as prescribed, and cover up at dusk. Our guide to [malaria, vaccines and health in Africa](/stories/malaria-vaccines-health-africa/) has the full picture.

## Before you book

Check the FCDO travel advice for every country on your route; South Africa held local government elections on 4 November 2026, and the FCDO's page was updated on 5 November to note possible increased political activity ([FCDO South Africa warnings](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/south-africa/warnings-and-insurance), checked November 2026). Then pick your season, decide between Kruger and the Okavango, and start with the Falls in mind. The roar is waiting.
