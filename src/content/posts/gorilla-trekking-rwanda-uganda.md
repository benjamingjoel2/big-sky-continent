It is still cool when you arrive, and the volcanoes are wrapped in cloud. Around you, people in gaiters and rain jackets are drinking coffee and pretending not to be nervous. A ranger with a clipboard is matching names to groups, and you catch the name of the gorilla family you will be looking for today. Somewhere up on those green slopes, a silverback is waking up, and he has no idea you are coming.

What follows is an honest walk through the day in Rwanda and Uganda, followed by the practical details you will need: permits, rules, fitness, safety and health.

## The morning briefing

In Rwanda, trekking happens in Volcanoes National Park. Visit Rwanda says visitors [gather at the park headquarters in Kinigi at 7am](https://visitrwanda.com/interests/gorilla-tracking/). Groups are assigned partly by fitness level, and everyone receives a briefing on the protocols and rules for the visit. Visit Rwanda says there are twelve habituated gorilla families in the park, with eight permits per family per day.

In Uganda, gorilla trekking takes place in Bwindi Impenetrable National Park and Mgahinga Gorilla National Park. The Uganda Wildlife Authority lists [four trekking sectors in Bwindi](https://ugandawildlife.org/activities/gorilla-tracking/): Buhoma, Ruhija, Rushaga and Nkuringo. This matters for planning, because your permit is for a specific sector, and the sectors can be far apart by road. Make sure your accommodation is near the sector on your permit. UWA's tariff lists a fine of half the permit fee for [tracking in a different location](https://ugandawildlife.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/UWA-Revised-Conservation-Tariff.pdf).

Listen properly at the briefing, and use it to ask about anything you are unsure of, including what to do if you feel unwell on the trail.

## The hike

Then you walk, out of the vehicle and up towards the forest, heading for wherever your family happens to be this morning.

How long it takes depends on where the family is that day. In Rwanda, Visit Rwanda says hikes [can last anything from 30 minutes to four or more hours](https://visitrwanda.com/interests/gorilla-tracking/), reaching altitudes between 2,500 and 4,000 metres. In Uganda, UWA says treks [last about 2 to 7 hours](https://ugandawildlife.org/activities/gorilla-tracking/) and need moderate fitness. UWA gives Bwindi's altitude as [1,160 to 2,607 metres above sea level](https://ugandawildlife.org/national-parks/bwindi-impenetrable-national-park/).

Altitude is real here. The UK government notes that [altitude sickness is a risk in parts of Rwanda](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/rwanda/health) (checked November 2026). Walk slowly, drink water, and tell your guide early if you feel unwell.

The forest itself is the other challenge: expect steep, muddy slopes, thick vegetation and stinging nettles.

## Porters: say yes

At the start of the trail you will meet local porters. Visit Rwanda says [porters are available](https://visitrwanda.com/interests/gorilla-tracking/) to carry backpacks and cameras and to lend a hand along the route. Hiring one is not a sign of weakness. It frees you to concentrate on your footing and gives you a steadying hand on slippery slopes. Ask at the briefing what the fee is and what is usual by way of a tip, and carry some cash.

## Your hour with the gorillas

Then, suddenly, you are there. Your guide signals for quiet. You step forward through the leaves, and there is a gorilla, eating, utterly unbothered by you. Often you hear them before you see them: the crunch of stems, a grunt, the soft thump of a youngster tumbling down a bank.

Both countries limit the time. Visit Rwanda says each family is visited for [a strict maximum of one hour per day](https://visitrwanda.com/interests/gorilla-tracking/), and UWA gives one hour of observation in Uganda. The hour goes very fast. Try to spend some of it with the camera down.

The rules exist to protect the gorillas, which can catch human illnesses. UWA's published gorilla tracking rules, printed in its [2024 to 2026 tariff](https://ugandawildlife.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/UWA-Conservation-Tariff-July-2024-June-2026.pdf), say:

- do not visit the gorillas if you have a cold or other infectious illness
- a maximum of eight people per gorilla group
- keep a 10 metre distance from the gorillas
- no smoking, eating or drinking during tracking
- if you must sneeze or cough, cover your face and turn away from the gorillas
- visits are limited to one hour per group per day
- no flash photography
- do not leave litter in the forest

Rwanda's rules are similar in spirit, and your guide will brief you on the current distance and on whether you are asked to wear a face mask near the gorillas. Follow their instructions on the day.

If you wake up with a cold on trekking day, tell your guide or operator. It is a hard thing to do after paying for a permit, but it is the right one.

## What to wear and how fit you need to be

Visit Rwanda calls [solid walking shoes with gaiters and sturdy clothing](https://visitrwanda.com/interests/gorilla-tracking/) to protect against stinging nettles essential, and suggests gloves and a light waterproof jacket. For Volcanoes National Park, it says that [in case of rain, gaiters and a lightweight waterproof](https://visitrwanda.com/destinations/volcanoes-national-park/) will come in useful.

In practice: long trousers tucked into gaiters, long sleeves, broken-in boots with good grip, gloves, a rain jacket, water, and layers for a cool start at altitude.

On fitness, you do not need to be an athlete, but you do need to be able to walk uphill on rough ground for several hours, possibly at altitude. Some hill walking in the weeks before helps.

## Permits: Rwanda

Visit Rwanda gives the gorilla permit price as [US$1,500 per person per day](https://visitrwanda.com/interests/gorilla-tracking/) (checked November 2026). It recommends booking in advance through the Irembo platform, or through a tour operator. The Volcanoes National Park page on Visit Rwanda also links to the Rwanda Development Board's own booking portal. With a fixed number of permits per family each day, popular dates can go early, so book well ahead.

## Permits: Uganda

The Uganda Wildlife Authority's [2024 to 2026 tariff](https://ugandawildlife.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/UWA-Conservation-Tariff-July-2024-June-2026.pdf) listed a gorilla permit at US$800 for foreign non-residents, and said that all gorilla and chimpanzee permits for foreign non-residents and visitors from the rest of Africa can only be booked through licensed tour operators.

UWA has since published a [conservation tariff for January 2027 to June 2028](https://ugandawildlife.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/UWA-Revised-Conservation-Tariff.pdf) (checked November 2026). It lists the gorilla permit for foreign non-residents at US$800, including the guide fee, park entrance for the day and a community development contribution. It also lists a lower low-season rate of US$600 for foreign non-residents, which it says applies only in April, May and November. UWA's tariff periods do not always run back to back, so confirm with UWA or your licensed operator which rate applies on your dates before you pay.

## Minimum age and the habituation experience

Both countries set a minimum age of 15 for gorilla trekking: Visit Rwanda says the [minimum age is 15 years](https://visitrwanda.com/interests/gorilla-tracking/), and UWA's rules say the same. If you are travelling as a family with younger children, plan other activities for them on trekking day.

In Bwindi, UWA also offers a gorilla habituation experience, a separate activity with its own permit. It involves joining a gorilla group that is still being accustomed to people. UWA's January 2027 to June 2028 tariff lists it at US$1,800 for foreign non-residents. Ask your operator what the day involves before you book.

## Golden monkeys and chimpanzees

Gorillas are not the only primates worth a morning. In Volcanoes National Park, Visit Rwanda describes golden monkeys, named for their bright orange bodies, as [more elusive than the gorillas](https://visitrwanda.com/itineraries/national-park-trio-lake-kivu/). In Uganda, UWA lists golden monkey tracking in Mgahinga in its tariff.

For chimpanzees, Rwanda's Nyungwe National Park is the place. Visit Rwanda says a [chimp trek there](https://visitrwanda.com/itineraries/national-park-trio-lake-kivu/) can last from one to four hours, and that once you find them you spend about an hour watching. In Uganda, UWA describes [Kibale National Park](https://ugandawildlife.org/national-parks/kibale-national-park/) as home to 13 primate species, with chimpanzee treks lasting roughly two to five hours, an hour with the chimps once they are found, and groups capped at six. UWA's 2027 to 2028 tariff lists chimpanzee tracking in Kibale at US$250 for foreign non-residents.

## Adding a savanna safari

Gorillas pair naturally with big open country. In Rwanda, Visit Rwanda describes Akagera National Park as having [some of the most scenic savannah in East Africa](https://visitrwanda.com/itineraries/national-park-trio-lake-kivu/), and its suggested itinerary links Akagera, Nyungwe, Lake Kivu and Volcanoes in a single circuit. You can also pair gorillas with a safari in Kenya or Tanzania. If that appeals, start with [your first African safari](/stories/first-african-safari/).

## Safety: check the border advice

Both gorilla areas lie close to the Democratic Republic of the Congo, so check the UK government's advice before you book and again before you travel.

For Rwanda, the [FCDO advises against all but essential travel](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/rwanda) to the parts of Rusizi District within 10km of the DRC border, including the Rwandan islands in Lake Kivu and Kamembe Airport. It says that if you plan to travel close to the DRC border on a gorilla or chimpanzee trek, you should [check the advice for Rusizi District and the DRC border region](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/rwanda/safety-and-security), and warns that border crossings could close at short notice (checked November 2026).

For Uganda, the FCDO notes that Bwindi and Mgahinga are in the extreme south-west of the country, and says [some gorilla trekking operators cross into DRC; avoid taking these tours](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/uganda/regional-risks). The FCDO advises against all travel to the provinces of DRC that border Uganda, including Virunga National Park. Its [safety and security page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/uganda/safety-and-security) also notes that national parks have been among the places targeted by past attacks in Uganda (checked November 2026). Read the [full Uganda advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/uganda), make sure your travel insurance covers your itinerary, and re-check both countries' advice shortly before you fly.

## Health: yellow fever and more

Yellow fever rules differ between the two countries, and they have changed recently, so check them close to your trip.

For Rwanda, the FCDO says you need [proof of yellow fever vaccination](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/rwanda/entry-requirements) if you are coming from a country listed as a transmission risk. TravelHealthPro says a certificate is [required for travellers over 1 year of age](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/countries/rwanda) arriving from countries with a risk of yellow fever transmission, while vaccination is generally not recommended for travel to Rwanda itself (checked November 2026).

For Uganda, TravelHealthPro says that, according to Uganda's Ministry of Internal Affairs as of October 2026, there are [no certificate requirements under International Health Regulations](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/countries/uganda), but that there is a risk of yellow fever transmission throughout Uganda and vaccination is recommended for travellers aged 9 months and older (checked November 2026). Because TravelHealthPro lists Uganda as a yellow fever risk country, a combined trip that goes from Uganda into Rwanda may mean you need a certificate for Rwanda. Ask a travel health clinic well before you go.

Also check the latest outbreak notices. The FCDO's [Uganda health page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/uganda/health) says an Ebola outbreak in Uganda has been declared over but that the outbreak in neighbouring DRC continues, and that you may face screening at entry points. Its [Rwanda health page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/rwanda/health) mentions heightened health screening at borders in the region (both checked November 2026).

For malaria, vaccines and how to prepare, read our guide to [malaria, vaccines and health in Africa](/stories/malaria-vaccines-health-africa/), and see a travel health professional well before you travel.

## The short version

Book early, sleep near your trailhead, hire a porter, dress for nettles and rain, and stay home from the forest if you are ill. Then let the hour happen. You will not remember the mud.
