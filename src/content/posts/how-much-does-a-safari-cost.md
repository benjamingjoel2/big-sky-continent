The engine cuts out and the silence arrives all at once. Below the vehicle, the river runs brown and slow, and a family of elephants has come down to drink: the big cows first, ears loose, then a calf pushing between their legs to reach the water. Somebody in the back seat forgets to lift their camera. For a few minutes nobody says anything at all.

That is what you are paying for. The trouble is that two safaris offering moments like this can cost wildly different amounts, and it is rarely obvious why. This guide does not give you a price, because honest prices depend on dates, places and choices that only you can make. Instead it explains what moves the number up and down, so you can read a quote with confidence and spend where it matters to you.

Official fees mentioned below were checked in November 2026. Fees and entry rules change, so always confirm the current figure with the authority before you book.

## The fixed costs you cannot negotiate

Some parts of the price are set by governments, not by whoever builds your trip.

**Park and conservation fees.** Most national parks charge per person, and often per day. Tanzania National Parks (TANAPA) publishes its tariffs as official documents: its [2023 to 2024 tariff](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/uploads/publications/en-1688147569-TARIFFS%20Design%20202324.pdf), for example, sets conservation fees per person per day that vary by park, by age, by citizenship or residency and, in the busiest northern parks, by season. The same document also lists hotel concession fees, vehicle entry fees and aircraft landing fees. We could not find a newer tariff on the [TANAPA website](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/) when we checked in November 2026, so we are not quoting figures: ask for the current rates in your quote. The point for budgeting is simple. Every extra day inside a park adds fees for every person in your group.

**Primate permits.** Gorilla trekking is the clearest example of a fixed, published price. The Rwanda Development Board's Visit Rwanda site lists a gorilla permit in Volcanoes National Park at [US$1,500 per person per day](https://visitrwanda.com/interests/gorilla-tracking/), for trekkers aged 15 and over (checked November 2026). Uganda Wildlife Authority sets its own gorilla permit prices in a published conservation tariff; the version on [its website](https://ugandawildlife.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/UWA-Conservation-Tariff-July-2024-June-2026.pdf) covers July 2024 to June 2026, so check with UWA for the rate that applies to your dates. Our guide to [gorilla trekking in Rwanda and Uganda](/stories/gorilla-trekking-rwanda-uganda/) covers the choice between the two.

**Visas and travel authorisations.** Kenya asks visitors to [apply online for an Electronic Travel Authorisation](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/kenya/entry-requirements) before travel through the [official eTA portal](https://www.etakenya.go.ke/en), and GOV.UK says you [must have a visa to enter Tanzania](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/tanzania/entry-requirements), with an official e-visa site (checked November 2026). Fees are set by each government and do change, so take the current figure from the official portal at the time you apply, never from a third-party site.

## Season: the biggest single lever

The same tent, on the same riverbank, can cost very different amounts depending on the month. Most camps price by season, typically with a peak season when the weather is driest and the wildlife most concentrated, and a quieter "green" or low season when rains return, the landscape turns lush and rates usually fall. Shoulder months sit in between.

Even official fees can follow the seasons: TANAPA's tariff sets different conservation fees for peak and low seasons in some parks. In Rwanda, a January 2026 Rwanda Development Board notice on reduced gorilla permit rates for African citizens and residents [excluded June to October](https://www.ktpress.rw/2026/01/rwanda-floats-promotional-gorilla-trekking-rates-into-2026/), described in the report as the peak tourism season.

Green season is not a compromise for everyone. You may trade a few rained-out afternoons for fewer vehicles at a sighting, newborn animals and dramatic skies. But some camps and roads close in the wettest months, so ask what is open.

## How you travel and who you travel with

**Shared or private vehicle.** Joining a shared game-drive vehicle, or a small group departure, spreads the cost of a guide and vehicle across more people. A private vehicle costs more but gives you control: you choose how long to stay with the elephants, and when to head back.

**Group size.** Many costs, such as a vehicle, a guide or a private plane charter, are per vehicle rather than per person. Travel as a family or with friends and those costs divide; travel as a couple and they do not.

**Road or light aircraft.** Driving between parks is usually cheaper and lets you see the country in between. Short flights in light aircraft save long days on the road and reach remote areas, but they add cost. Ask about luggage limits before you pack.

**Single travellers.** Rooms and tents are usually priced for two. Many lodges add a single supplement if you travel alone, though some waive it at certain times of year. Ask.

## Where you sleep, and where that is

**Lodge style.** At one end are budget camping safaris, with simple tents and a cook travelling with you. Then come permanent tented camps, larger lodges, and seasonal mobile camps that move to follow the wildlife. Generally, the smaller, more remote and more exclusive the camp, the higher the rate, because supplying and staffing it costs more.

**National park or private land.** Inside a national park, you share the roads with everyone else who has paid the fee. Private reserves and concessions, often next to the parks, limit how many vehicles and guests can be on the land, and may allow activities parks do not, such as night drives or walking. That exclusivity is usually reflected in the price.

**Botswana's approach.** Botswana has long been associated with low-impact, low-density safaris. A 2023 academic review notes that the country's first tourism policy, in 1990, [advocated a high-cost, low-volume approach](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/botswanas-new-tourism-policy-of-2021-and-mantras-of-citizen-empowerment-and-product-diversification-a-case-of-going-backward-to-move-forward), and that a revised 2021 policy moves towards a mixed-price strategy. Where a camp limits its guest numbers, expect that exclusivity to show in the price, and ask how many guests and vehicles share the area.

## The costs people forget

**International flights.** Often one of the larger items, and outside most safari quotes. Prices move with season and school holidays as much as safari rates do.

**Travel insurance.** The UK government's Tanzania advice says to check you have [appropriate travel insurance for local treatment or unexpected medical evacuation](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/tanzania/health). Its [insurance guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/foreign-travel-insurance) warns that failing to declare a health condition may invalidate your cover and that you should check every activity is included (checked November 2026). Remote camps make evacuation cover essential, not optional.

**Vaccines and health.** Travel vaccines, antimalarial tablets and a clinic appointment all cost money, and some are not free. Our guide to [malaria, vaccines and staying healthy in Africa](/stories/malaria-vaccines-health-africa/) explains what to expect.

**Tips and extras.** Ask whoever books your trip whether tips for guides and camp staff are expected or included, and budget for them separately if not. Drinks, laundry, spa treatments and optional activities such as balloon flights may or may not be included.

## Where to save, and where not to

**Good places to save:**

- **Travel in shoulder or green season**, if the trade-offs suit you.
- **Mix your stays.** One or two nights somewhere special, framed by simpler camps or lodges, often gives you most of the magic for less.
- **Fewer places, longer stays.** Every move between camps adds transfer time and, often, cost. Staying put also gives you more chances at good sightings.
- **Share a vehicle**, or travel as a larger group.
- **Drive rather than fly** where the distances are reasonable.

**Places not to cut corners:**

- **Guiding.** A skilled guide shapes every hour of your safari: what you find, what you understand, and how safely you watch it.
- **Location.** A cheaper camp a long drive from the wildlife can cost you hours each day. Ask how far you are from the main game-viewing areas.
- **Insurance with medical evacuation.**
- **Time.** A trip squeezed into too few days spends much of its budget on getting there.

## What to ask for in a quote

When you compare quotes, make sure you are comparing the same thing. Ask, in writing:

1. **Are all park, conservation and concession fees included,** at the rates for my travel dates? Who pays if they rise before I travel?
2. **Which transfers are included?** Airport pick-ups, road transfers between parks and any light-aircraft flights.
3. **What is included at each camp?** Meals, drinks (soft drinks, house wine, premium spirits), laundry, and which activities: game drives, walks, boat trips, night drives.
4. **Is the vehicle private or shared?** How many guests per vehicle, and is there a window seat for everyone?
5. **Are permits booked and confirmed?** For gorilla or chimpanzee treks, ask for confirmation that permits are secured, not just requested.
6. **What are the payment terms?** Deposit, final balance date, accepted currencies and card charges.
7. **What are the cancellation terms,** and what happens if a camp or flight changes?
8. **How is my money protected?** In the UK, if you book a package that includes a flight, [ATOL protection](https://www.atol.org/about-atol/what-does-atol-protection-mean/) means you should receive an ATOL Certificate, and the ATOL scheme helps if the company fails, for example by helping you complete your trip or get home. ATOL is not travel insurance. You can check whether a company holds an ATOL on the ATOL website (checked November 2026). If your package does not include flights, ask the company to explain in writing how your payments are protected.
9. **What is not included?** International flights, visas, insurance, tips and personal extras are often outside the price. A clear list now avoids surprises later.

## Back at the river

The elephants finish drinking and move off into the mopane, the calf trotting to keep up. Later, back at camp, nobody talks about what the day cost. They talk about the calf.

Budget well, and that is how your trip will feel too: not a list of fees, but a handful of moments you were in exactly the right place for. If this is your first time, our guide to [planning your first African safari](/stories/first-african-safari/) is a good place to start.
