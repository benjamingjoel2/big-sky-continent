Late afternoon in Amboseli, and the dust is turning gold. A line of elephants walks across the flats towards the swamp, unhurried, the smallest calf tucked almost underneath its mother. Behind them, as if someone has hung it there for effect, the clouds part and Kilimanjaro appears: a long, pale shoulder of mountain with snow along the top. In Kenya, you are looking at Tanzania's mountain. And that is a neat way into the question so many first-timers ask.

Kenya or Tanzania? The honest answer is that both are superb, they share the same great ecosystem in places, and you cannot really go wrong. But they feel different, and one may suit you better. Here is how they compare.

## The parks: Kenya

**Masai Mara.** Kenya's most famous reserve is the northern part of the wider Mara-Serengeti ecosystem. It is a national reserve run by the [Narok County Government](https://ke.chm-cbd.net/protected-areas/maasai-mara-national-reserve), not by the Kenya Wildlife Service, with the Mara Triangle west of the Mara River managed on the county's behalf by the non-profit Mara Conservancy, according to the reserve's [management plan](https://narok.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/MNNR-Management-Plan-Final-February-2023-1_compressed.pdf). Big cats, open grassland and, in season, river crossings by the migrating herds. The same plan is candid that the reserve gets very busy at crossings and big-cat sightings in peak months.

**Amboseli.** A compact park of around [392 square kilometres](https://ke.chm-cbd.net/protected-areas/amboseli-national-park) known for big elephant herds and views of Kilimanjaro. The Kenya Wildlife Service calls it the ["Home of the African Elephant"](https://reservations.kws.go.ke/location/amboseli-national-park), with habitats ranging from the dry bed of Lake Amboseli to wetlands with sulphur springs.

**Samburu.** Up in northern Kenya, the Samburu, Buffalo Springs and Shaba reserves are managed by Isiolo County. Their drier country holds species that are [rarely found elsewhere in Kenya](https://ke.chm-cbd.net/protected-areas/samburu-buffalo-springs-and-shaba-national-reserves), such as Grevy's zebra and reticulated giraffe, which makes Samburu a lovely contrast to the Mara.

**Tsavo.** Tsavo East and Tsavo West together form [Kenya's largest protected area](https://ke.chm-cbd.net/protected-areas/tsavo-national-parks-tsavo-east-and-tsavo-west): big, wild, less visited country with the palm-lined Galana River and the long lava flow of the Yatta Plateau. It sits conveniently between Nairobi and the coast.

**Lake Nakuru.** A Rift Valley lake park with a fenced sanctuary protecting [both black and white rhinos](https://ke.chm-cbd.net/protected-areas/lake-nakuru-national-park). It is famous for flamingos, but their numbers rise and fall with water and food conditions, so treat them as a bonus rather than a promise.

## The parks: Tanzania

**Serengeti.** The name everyone knows. Tanzania National Parks describes the Serengeti's Great Migration as ["the World's last remaining large mammal migration"](https://tanzaniaparks.go.tz/serengeti/about), and the park has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site [since 1981](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/en/serengeti). It is vast, and the experience changes completely depending on which part you visit and when.

**Ngorongoro Crater.** A collapsed volcano roughly [20 kilometres across](https://www.ncaa.go.tz/ngorongoro-crater/), with a floor about 610 metres below the rim. It is managed by the Ngorongoro Conservation Area Authority rather than Tanzania National Parks, only four-wheel-drive vehicles may go down, and all crater visitors must be with [a licensed guide](https://www.ncaa.go.tz/visitor-guidelines-and-procedures/). Expect a crater service fee per vehicle on top of the normal entry permit; see the [NCAA tariffs page](https://www.ncaa.go.tz/tariffs/) for current figures.

**Tarangire.** Billed by the park authority as ["The Paradise of Elephants"](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/en/tarangire), with ancient baobabs and large herds gathering in the dry season.

**Lake Manyara.** A smaller park at the foot of the Rift Valley escarpment, with [groundwater forest, a soda lake and its famous tree-climbing lions](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/en/lake-manyara). It fits easily on the way to the crater.

**Ruaha.** Tanzania's [second-largest national park](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/en/ruaha), in the south-central part of the country, with the Great Ruaha River at its heart, both greater and lesser kudu, and walking safaris.

**Nyerere.** Carved from the old Selous landscape and covering [more than 30,000 square kilometres](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/en/nyerere), Nyerere is river country. Boat safaris on the Rufiji, drifting past hippos and crocodiles, are a highlight.

Tanzania's northern circuit (Serengeti, Ngorongoro, Tarangire, Manyara) is a classic first trip, while Ruaha and Nyerere in the south feel remoter and wilder.

## The Great Migration: how it splits

The wildebeest and zebra do not recognise the border. The Mara management plan describes an annual migration between the Mara, the Serengeti, the Ngorongoro Conservation Area and neighbouring community land, and notes that a smaller Kenyan migration mixes with it in the Mara [between July and September](https://narok.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/MNNR-Management-Plan-Final-February-2023-1_compressed.pdf).

In broad terms, Tanzania hosts the herds for most of the year. Serengeti park management says the wildebeest prefer to calve on [the short-grass plains between Naabi gate and the Ngorongoro highlands](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/uploads/publications/en-1724693797-MararivercrossingGuideline.pdf), with most calves born in a few weeks from mid-February. Kenya's moment is the dry-season stretch when the herds reach the Mara. River crossings on the Mara River can happen on both sides of the border, and the Serengeti now has designated viewing points, vehicle limits and a stay-in-your-vehicle rule at crossings.

Herds move with the rain, not the calendar, so be wary of anyone who guarantees a crossing. For the detail, see [the Great Migration month by month](/stories/great-migration-month-by-month/).

## Conservancies: Kenya's quiet advantage

One thing Kenya does especially well is the conservancy. Around the Mara, Maasai landowners lease their land into [community conservancies](https://maraconservancies.org/mara-conservancies/) in partnership with tourism operators, and the lease income goes to the families who own the land. Some conservancies strictly monitor visitor numbers, and some offer walking or horse riding.

That matters for your experience. The Mara reserve's management plan says walking safaris, night drives and horseback safaris [are not permitted inside the reserve](https://narok.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/MNNR-Management-Plan-Final-February-2023-1_compressed.pdf), and notes that the conservancies to the north are aiming for lower visitor numbers. Many first-timers combine a few nights in a conservancy with day trips into the reserve.

## Getting around: road or light aircraft

Both countries can be done by road, by air, or a mix.

In Kenya, distances between the main parks are manageable by road, and the Mara has [six airstrips](https://narok.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/MNNR-Management-Plan-Final-February-2023-1_compressed.pdf) inside the reserve for light aircraft. In Tanzania, the northern circuit is often done as a road safari from Arusha, but distances grow once you add the Serengeti: Tanzania National Parks says the road journey can take [seven to eight hours](https://tanzaniaparks.go.tz/serengeti/visitor-guide), with regular flights to Seronera airstrip as the alternative. In the south, Nyerere is usually reached by [flights from Dar es Salaam](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/en/nyerere).

Road safaris let you see the country in between, villages and markets and all. Flying saves time and backs, but costs more. Whichever you choose, the Foreign Office advises [avoiding driving at night](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/kenya/safety-and-security) in Kenya where possible.

## Beaches: the Kenyan coast or Zanzibar

Both countries have the Indian Ocean on their doorstep. In Kenya, the beach means the mainland coast; in Tanzania, most people head across the water to the islands of Zanzibar. Either makes a lovely way to unwind after a week of early starts in the bush, and both are easy to add on to a safari. One practical point for Zanzibar: if it is your first point of entry into Tanzania, the insurance rules described below apply there. Our guide to [safari and beach combinations](/stories/safari-and-beach-combinations/) compares the options.

## Kilimanjaro

If you would like to climb as well as look, Tanzania wins. Kilimanjaro rises to [5,895 metres](https://www.tanzaniaparks.go.tz/en/kilimanjaro), Africa's highest peak, and Tanzania National Parks lists six summit routes taking five to eight days, noting that longer routes give a better chance of reaching the top. In Kenya you get the view, especially from Amboseli, but the mountain itself is climbed from the Tanzanian side.

## Entry requirements

Both countries need paperwork before you fly, and the rules depend on your nationality. The details below are for British passport holders, checked November 2026. Please re-check before you travel.

**Kenya.** You must apply online for an [Electronic Travel Authorisation](https://www.etakenya.go.ke/) before you travel, and the Foreign Office says the local authorities recommend applying [at least two weeks before departure](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/kenya/entry-requirements). Your passport needs at least six months' validity from arrival and two blank pages. From 5 October 2026, most visitors staying less than 12 months must have Kenyan government-provided travel health insurance, bought as part of the eTA application.

**Tanzania.** You need a visa. The Foreign Office directs travellers to apply in advance through the official [Tanzania e-visa portal](https://visa.immigration.go.tz/), notes that single-entry visas can also be obtained on arrival at main ports, and says your passport needs six months' validity and one blank page. Visitors must also have [mandatory Tanzanian government-provided inbound travel insurance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/tanzania/entry-requirements); if Zanzibar is your first point of entry, you buy it from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation.

For both, you need proof of yellow fever vaccination if you are arriving from a country with a risk of yellow fever transmission. Always use the official portals rather than look-alike sites, and if you hold a different passport, check your own government's advice.

## Which suits you?

**Choose Kenya if** you want a shorter trip with less time on the road, you love the idea of a conservancy with fewer vehicles and the chance to walk, you would like elephants under Kilimanjaro at Amboseli, or you want northern species in Samburu.

**Choose Tanzania if** you want the Serengeti's scale, the Ngorongoro Crater, the calving season on the southern plains early in the year, or a wilder southern safari in Ruaha or Nyerere. Choose it too if you want to climb Kilimanjaro or finish on Zanzibar.

**Choose both if** you have the time. The two countries share the Migration and a border, and plenty of trips cross from one to the other.

Whichever you pick, use a reputable, licensed operator, never buy from touts, and [follow park regulations and advice from wardens](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/tanzania/safety-and-security).
