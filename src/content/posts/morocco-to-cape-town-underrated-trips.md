The light goes first. One minute the dunes are the colour of paprika, ridged and sharp against a hard blue sky; the next they are soft and violet, and the only sound is sand hissing off a crest in the wind. Somewhere below, a camel groans as it folds itself down for the night. You came here because someone said Morocco was all souks and riads. Nobody mentioned this.

Africa is full of trips like that: places that sit just outside the familiar circuit of safari parks and beach resorts, and reward you all the more for it. What follows is a journey from north to south, one country at a time, through the trips we think deserve far more attention. Each comes with what makes it special, a sense of when to go where the official sources say, and one honest practical note.

A word before you start. Safety and entry rules change, sometimes quickly, and several UK government pages currently carry Ebola-related entry and screening notes. Every safety point below links to the relevant UK FCDO page, checked in November 2026. Re-check them before you book and again before you fly.

## Morocco, beyond Marrakech

Marrakech is wonderful, but Morocco's quieter corners are where the country really opens up. In Fes, the walled medina is recognised by UNESCO, and the official tourism board suggests entering through Bab Boujloud and losing yourself in the streets of Fes el-Bali, past the leather tanneries and the Quaraouiyine Mosque ([Visit Morocco](https://www.visitmorocco.com/en/travel/fez)).

Cross the Atlas and you reach Merzouga, which Visit Morocco calls "one of the gates of the Sahara", a village in the middle of the sands beside the dunes of Erg Chebbi. A dromedary caravan and a night in a desert bivouac is the classic way to experience it, and the tourism board says the desert is best visited from October to April, since summer is very hot and winter nights are cold ([Visit Morocco](https://www.visitmorocco.com/en/travel/errachidia-midelt-merzouga)). Finish on the Atlantic in Essaouira, the "Bride of the Atlantic", with its working fishing port, its wind-loving surfers and the Gnaoua festival each summer ([Visit Morocco](https://www.visitmorocco.com/en/travel/essaouira-mogador)).

**Practical note:** the FCDO warns that bogus guides often approach tourists in medinas and advises using only guides with an official badge. If you trek in the Atlas, it recommends a professional guide and warns of flash flooding in the mountains in winter ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/morocco/safety-and-security)).

## Senegal: river towns and mangrove creeks

Senegal moves to its own rhythm. Saint-Louis, in the north, has been a UNESCO World Heritage site since 2000, and the Faidherbe bridge is the only crossing point to the historic island at its heart ([Senegal Tourism](https://www.visitezlesenegal.com/en/saint-louis/)). The same official page points birdwatchers to the nearby Djoudj park.

Further south, the Sine Saloum delta is a maze of mangroves, creeks and lagoons, with shell islands and bird islands to visit by canoe. Senegal's tourism board describes it as a UNESCO World Heritage site and says it is home to some 400 bird species ([Senegal Tourism](https://www.visitezlesenegal.com/en/destinations/sine-saloum/)). Off Dakar, the island of Gorée was inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1978. UNESCO describes its House of Slaves as a holding centre for enslaved people before they were shipped to the Americas, and the island as a flagship destination for memorial tourism ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/goree-island-memory)). It is a visit to take slowly.

**Practical note:** the FCDO does not currently advise against travel to any part of Senegal, but asks travellers to take particular care in remote areas near the Mauritania and Mali borders ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/senegal/regional-risks)). It also warns that driving after dark is more dangerous because of poorly lit roads ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/senegal/safety-and-security)).

## Ghana: the coast and the court

Ghana's coastline carries some of the heaviest history on the continent. UNESCO recognises the country's coastal forts and castles together as one cultural World Heritage site ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/historic-visit-ghanas-unesco-heritage-castles)). At Cape Coast Castle, a guided tour takes you through the slave dungeons and Dalzel Tower, and the West African Historical Museum inside holds shackles from the slave trade ([Visit Ghana](https://visitghana.com/?p=12527)). St George's Castle at Elmina is nearby.

Inland, Kumasi is the seat of the Asante kingdom. The Manhyia Palace Museum, in the grounds of Manhyia Palace, was created to commemorate the Asante leaders ([Visit Ghana](https://visitghana.com/manhyia-palace-museum/)), and the Akwasidae festival is celebrated every six weeks on Sundays at the palace, honouring the ancestors and the Asantehene ([Visit Ghana](https://visitghana.com/akwasidae-festival/)). Time your visit to coincide if you can.

**Practical note:** the only area the FCDO advises against (all but essential travel) is Bawku Municipality in the Upper East region ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ghana)). It warns of dangerous rip tides along the southern coast and advises against road travel outside main towns after dark ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ghana/safety-and-security)).

## São Tomé and Príncipe: islands on the equator

If you want to feel genuinely far from the crowds, this small island nation in the Gulf of Guinea is the place. The whole of Príncipe, with nearby islets and the surrounding sea, is a UNESCO biosphere reserve, designated in 2012. UNESCO highlights its high rates of endemism in plants, birds, reptiles and more, a mountainous south rising to 948 metres, and surrounding waters that support sea turtles, seabirds, cetaceans and coral reefs ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/mab/island-principe)). Expect forest, quiet beaches and a pace that slows right down.

**Practical note:** UK citizens can visit for tourism for up to 15 days without a visa and pay an entry fee on arrival ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/sao-tome-and-principe/entry-requirements)). The FCDO says crime is generally low, but many roads are in poor condition and unlit, and taxis and motorbike taxis are the only public transport ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/sao-tome-and-principe/safety-and-security)).

## Ethiopia: a trip to keep on the list

Few places on earth match northern Ethiopia. At Lalibela, eleven monolithic churches were carved in the 13th century directly from living volcanic rock, and the site was inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1978 ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/lalibela-heart-cultural-diplomacy-and-world-heritage-conservation)). In the Simien Mountains, jagged peaks and sheer precipices drop some 1,500 metres, and the park protects the Walia ibex, found nowhere else in the world, along with the Ethiopian wolf and the gelada ([IUCN World Heritage Outlook](https://worldheritageoutlook.iucn.org/explore-sites/simien-national-park)).

**Practical note:** both lie in Amhara region, and the FCDO currently advises against all travel to Amhara region, as well as to Tigray, Gambela and several border areas ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ethiopia/warnings-and-insurance)). For now, this is one to read about, plan for and keep watching, not one to book. Check the advice regularly and make sure any future insurance covers your full itinerary.

## Zambia: Africa on foot

Zambia is where you swap the vehicle for your own two feet. Zambia Tourism says the walking safari originated in South Luangwa, a valley "aptly named the Valley of the Leopard", with the Luangwa River as the park's life-blood ([Zambia Tourism](https://www.zambiatourism.com/destinations/national-parks/south-luangwa-national-park/)). Walking slows everything down: you notice tracks, birdsong and the smell of the bush in a way no game drive allows.

The same page says the dry season runs from April to October, with warm sunny days and chilly nights from May to August, while the wet season from November to March brings migrant birds. Mfuwe International Airport, just outside the park, is the hub for flights.

**Practical note:** British passport holders do not need a visa for Zambia, but the FCDO notes Zambia may introduce health screening for travellers arriving from Ebola-affected regions ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/zambia/entry-requirements)). It also says it is dangerous to drive outside the main towns after dark ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/zambia/safety-and-security)), so flying in to Mfuwe makes sense.

## Malawi: a freshwater sea

Lake Malawi is so large it feels like an ocean, but the water is fresh and astonishingly clear. Lake Malawi National Park, at the southern end of the lake, was inscribed as a World Heritage site in 1984. IUCN notes that more than 99 per cent of its cichlid fish occur only in Lake Malawi, and that their rapid speciation is highly significant for the study of evolution ([IUCN World Heritage Outlook](https://worldheritageoutlook.iucn.org/explore-sites/lake-malawi-national-park)). Snorkelling over the rocks among them is unforgettable. The same assessment rates the park's outlook as of significant concern, mainly because of overfishing and pollution, so choose low-impact activities.

**Practical note:** UK visitors need a visa, available on arrival or as an e-visa, and must declare foreign cash on arrival ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malawi/entry-requirements)). The FCDO warns of frequent fuel shortages and advises against driving at night ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malawi/safety-and-security)), so build slack into any road trip.

## Madagascar: evolution's own island

Madagascar feels like another planet. Its lemurs are endemic to the island, and IUCN warns that 98 per cent of lemur species face extinction risk ([IUCN](https://iucn.org/news/202510/world-lemur-day-reflecting-progress-and-path-ahead-sos-lemurs)). The Rainforests of the Atsinanana, a World Heritage site made up of six national parks along the east, including Ranomafana and Andringitra, are home to all five lemur families, and nearly all their reptiles and amphibians are found only on Madagascar ([IUCN World Heritage Outlook](https://worldheritageoutlook.iucn.org/explore-sites/wdpaid/903062)). Hearing lemurs call through the forest at dawn is reason enough to go.

**Practical note:** the FCDO says the cyclone season normally runs from November to April and the December to April rains can leave secondary roads passable only by 4x4. It advises against driving at night and warns of armed robberies on main roads, particularly after dark ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/madagascar/safety-and-security)). Madagascar has also introduced Ebola-related health measures for some arrivals ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/madagascar/entry-requirements)).

## Namibia: the fog desert

The journey ends in the Namib. The Namib Sand Sea, inscribed as a World Heritage site in 2013, is described by IUCN as the only coastal desert in the world with extensive dune fields influenced by fog, and its scenic dunes around Sossusvlei are the most visited part ([IUCN World Heritage Outlook](https://worldheritageoutlook.iucn.org/node/1175)). Climb a dune at first light and you will understand why.

**Practical note:** UK nationals have needed a visa for Namibia since 1 April 2025 ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/namibia/entry-requirements)). The FCDO warns that accidents are common on gravel roads, advises against driving outside towns after dark and says gravel roads deteriorate in the January to April rains ([FCDO, checked November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/namibia/safety-and-security)).

From the Namib, the road runs on south towards South Africa and, eventually, Cape Town. If you want to keep going, our [14-day southern Africa itinerary](/stories/a-14-day-southern-africa-itinerary/) picks up the road from there.
